In short: the base case (paid ads, a handful of creator partnerships, R35 000 a month on marketing) reaches about 499 paying customers and R381 501 in monthly subscriptions by the end of February, turns a monthly profit in January despite the festive shutdown, and has paid back its launch costs by February.   • Small businesses: about 2 700 000 SMMEs, roughly a third formal (SEDA, Absa Small Business Growth Index 2025). We count the 60% of formal businesses assumed to market on social media.
  • Creators: one South African creator network alone counts over 500 000 creators (theSalt); we count the 30% assumed to post weekly and want to grow or earn.
  • Agencies: 31 000–37 000 registered advertising and marketing firms (CIPC data via CompanyData and InfobelPRO); we count the 35% assumed to run clients’ social media.
  • Audience: almost 41 million South Africans are active on social media (Ornico / World Wide Worx 2026), so the people these customers market to are there.

The serviceable market is about 700 850 potential customers. Even the optimistic scenario ends February with 0.27% of it paying, so the forecast is limited by marketing reach and conversion, not by market size. Long-term, a 8 834-customer base at today’s plan mix is the 3-year ceiling these shares imply.

6. What this means

  • Marketing drives everything. Organic-only (conservative) loses about R41 548 over the three months and reaches only 138 paying customers. Spending R35 000 a month (base) is profitable from January.
  • December will lose money in every scenario (launch costs plus the festive slowdown). Plan cash for about R59 148 before revenue catches up.
  • Trial clip projects are limited (done). Since 24 October a trial turns videos of up to 30 minutes into clips with the fast speech model, like Creator Starter, instead of Agency’s 2 hours. With the old limit the 3-month result would have been −R59 687 (conservative), R42 765 (base), R733 732 (optimistic), and clip work at the busiest hour about five times higher.
  • The servers must scale before launch. At the busiest hour in December the base case already sends 2.1× more render work and 2× more clip work than today’s one-at-a-time queues can finish (16× with the old 2-hour trial). The worker pool in the readiness report (docs/capacity/KenganSocial_1000_Users_Readiness.pdf) is a launch requirement, not a later improvement. By February the base case needs about 20 workers at peak (already included in the costs above).
  • Agency customers carry the profit. In the base case Agency is ~10% of new paying customers but about 46% of February’s MRR. Direct outreach to agencies is the highest-value marketing.
  • Not included: salaries and contractors, office costs, income tax, annual-plan cash paid upfront (it would improve cash flow), and any price changes. Exchange rate R17.50/US$.
