1. How the costs are measured

Every action is costed three ways and the plan limits are counted in the same units:

  • By the second: video work (transcription, analysis, rendering, exporting) is measured in VM-seconds: seconds of one 4-CPU server (e2-standard-4, Johannesburg). The server costs ~US$140/month; capacity is bought for peaks, so only 50.0% of every second is counted as useful. One useful VM-second costs R0,00189.
  • By execution: each call to an outside AI or media API is costed from the provider's price, at its worst case (largest output it is allowed to produce, most expensive model in the fallback chain).
  • By plan limits: a plan's cost is every allowance used in full, times the unit's worst-case cost.
rand cost of an API call = US$ price × R17.5 (exchange) × 1.15 (VAT on imported services) × 1.2 (safety buffer) net revenue = list price ÷ 1.15 (treated as VAT-inclusive) payment fee = (price × 3.2% + R2.00) × 1.15 gross margin = (net revenue − cost at 100% use − payment fee) ÷ net revenue ≥ 60%

Fixed monthly costRand
Remotion licence minimum ($100, render fees count towards it)R2 012
Backups, snapshots, domain, email, monitoringR600
TotalR2 613

The server itself is costed per VM-second inside each plan (at 50.0% utilisation); when usage grows, a second server is added and the per-second cost stays the same. Even if every customer used 100% of every allowance, the average paid plan contributes R2 325 a month, so the fixed costs are covered from about 15 paying customers on the cheapest plan (2 on an even mix). Typical customers use far less than 100%, so real break-even comes sooner.

If the rand weakens past that, change usdZar in the model and re-run: the pricing test then fails and points at the plan that needs a smaller allowance or a higher price.

7. What changed in the app

  • Limits (server and client planLimits.ts, pricing page, Billing): new allowances above; transcription counted in minutes.
  • Per-second counting: transcription by the minute everywhere (clips, repurpose, AI edit, standalone transcribe); editor exports and AI edits per 5 minutes of video.
  • Per-execution counting: captions per platform; AI images 5 or 6 credits everywhere (carousels, thumbnails, AI Writer); trending refresh, website set-up, thumbnail headlines, DM drafts and lyrics now cost credits, with refunds when the work fails or is served from cache.
  • AI Writer uses the plan's monthly AI credits; the old never-resetting 50-credit balance is gone. Its legacy video render uses the render allowance.
  • Trial keeps every Agency feature but with Creator Starter quantities.
  • Free starter clip daily cap 25 by default (FREE_CLIP_DAILY_CAP).
  • Guard rail: server/tests/security/pricing.test.ts checks the app's limits, prices and credit costs against this model and fails if any paid plan falls below 60%.

8. Findings still open

  • Prices to confirm with the providers: Treblo's per-track price is not published (estimated at $0.1); the lyrics model is costed at Opus 4.5/4.6 rates; Payfast recurring-card fees; the VM's exact Johannesburg price. Update scripts/pricing-model.mjs and re-run.
  • Measure on the real server: the VM-seconds per media second come from local runs and the deploy notes. Time a 10-minute clip project and a 25-s video ad on the VM and update COMPUTE.
  • Two legacy endpoints are still unmetered: /api/ai/video/analyze (Whisper base on uploads up to 100 MB) and /api/ai/video/edit. Their size cap limits the exposure; meter or retire them next.
  • Advertised but not enforced: the connected-accounts limit (2/3/6/25) and storage (only enforced for the Agency library).
  • Fallbacks are cheaper, not dearer: when a cheaper provider answers (Gemini Flash, Pollinations' free images), real costs are below this model. The worst case is what the margin is guaranteed on.
